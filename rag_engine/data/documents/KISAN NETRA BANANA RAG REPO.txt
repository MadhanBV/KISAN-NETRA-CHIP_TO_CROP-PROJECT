	1.2 What is banana anthracnose?
Banana anthracnose is a fungal disease that primarily affects banana fruits. It can develop while fruits are growing in the field, but symptoms often become more visible during ripening, transportation and storage.
The fungus may infect young fruits without producing immediately visible symptoms. This is called a latent infection.
As the banana ripens, the infection can become active, producing dark, sunken lesions on the peel. Under humid conditions, fungal spores may develop on the affected areas.
The disease is particularly important for farmers, traders and exporters because it reduces fruit quality and market value.
1.3 Symptoms and identification
EARLY STAGE
* Small brown or black spots appear on the banana peel.
* Initial lesions may be circular.
* Spots may be difficult to notice on green fruits.
* Infected areas may become slightly discoloured.
* Fruits may appear healthy externally despite latent infection.
MODERATE STAGE
* Dark brown or black lesions become larger.
* Affected areas become sunken.
* Lesions may develop around damaged portions of the peel.
* Multiple spots may begin merging.
* Orange or salmon-coloured spore masses may appear under humid conditions.
SEVERE STAGE
* Large black and sunken lesions develop.
* Multiple infected areas merge.
* The peel becomes damaged and shrivelled.
* Fruit quality and appearance deteriorate.
* Advanced infections may extend into the pulp and cause fruit decay.
1.4 Causal organism
The major causal organism is Colletotrichum musae.
Important characteristics:
* It belongs to the fungal genus Colletotrichum.
* It produces spores that can spread through water splash and contaminated materials.
* It can infect fruit during field development.
* Infections may remain latent until the fruit begins ripening.
* High humidity and suitable temperatures favour disease development.
1.5 Disease development and transmission
1. Fungal spores are present on infected plant material and fruit surfaces.
2. Spores spread through rain splash, moisture and contaminated handling materials.
3. The fungus reaches the fruit surface.
4. Infection occurs through natural openings or damaged tissues.
5. The fungus may remain inactive or latent in young fruits.
6. As the fruit ripens, symptoms become visible.
7. Dark lesions expand and may merge under favourable conditions.

	1.6 Impact on farmers
* Reduced fruit quality.
* Lower market value.
* Increased post-harvest losses.
* Reduced shelf life.
* Rejection of affected fruits by retailers and exporters.
* Increased sorting, grading and packaging costs.
* Reduced suitability for long-distance transportation.
1.7 Prevention and management
A. Field management
* Maintain good plantation sanitation.
* Remove severely infected and decaying plant material.
* Avoid unnecessary fruit injuries.
* Maintain suitable spacing and canopy ventilation.
* Avoid excessive moisture around bunches.
* Use healthy planting material.
* Follow locally recommended bunch management practices.
* Monitor fruits regularly for early symptoms.
B. Harvesting management
* Harvest fruits carefully.
* Avoid cutting or bruising the banana fingers.
* Prevent bunches from touching contaminated soil.
* Use clean harvesting tools.
* Avoid dropping bunches during handling.
* Maintain appropriate maturity standards for the target market.
C. Post-harvest management
* Handle harvested bunches carefully.
* Remove damaged and visibly infected fruits.
* Use clean water and suitable approved sanitation procedures during washing.
* Clean and sanitise crates and packaging equipment.
* Maintain appropriate storage temperature and humidity.
* Avoid condensation and unnecessary wetness.
* Ensure good ventilation.
* Separate infected lots from healthy produce.
* Follow suitable ripening and transportation practices.
D. Chemical and biological management
* Use only currently registered and locally approved fungicides or post-harvest treatments.
* Verify that the product is approved for the specific crop, disease and application method.
* Follow label instructions, waiting periods and safety requirements.
* Do not use unverified chemical dip concentrations or household chemical mixtures.
* Biological treatments should be selected based on validated efficacy and local recommendations.

	1.9 Frequently asked questions
Q1. Is banana anthracnose a fungal disease?
Yes. It is mainly associated with Colletotrichum musae.
Q2. Can healthy-looking green bananas have anthracnose?
Yes. The fungus can cause latent infections that become visible during ripening.
Q3. Does banana anthracnose affect only the peel?
It primarily affects the peel, but advanced infections can extend into the pulp.
Q4. Does rain increase anthracnose risk?
Wet conditions and rain splash can favour infection and spore dispersal.
Q5. Can infected bananas be stored with healthy bananas?
It is better to segregate visibly infected fruits because they can deteriorate rapidly and contribute to contamination during handling.
Q6. Can washing remove anthracnose?
Washing may remove surface dirt and some surface contamination, but it cannot cure an established internal or latent infection.

3.2 Planting material
* Select healthy, disease-free suckers or quality-assured tissue-culture plants.
* Use varieties recommended for the local region.
* Inspect planting material for pests and diseases.
* Avoid damaged or infected suckers.
* Maintain proper spacing according to cultivar and cultivation system.

* Use drip irrigation where suitable.
* Avoid waterlogging.
* Increase monitoring during dry periods.
* Use mulching to reduce evaporation.
* Consider soil moisture sensors for efficient irrigation.
3.4 Nutrient management
* Conduct soil testing.
* Apply nutrients based on crop stage and local recommendations.
* Maintain balanced nitrogen and potassium nutrition.
* Use well-decomposed organic manure where suitable.
* Consider fertigation for efficient nutrient delivery.
* Avoid excessive fertiliser application.
3.5 Plantation management
* Remove unwanted suckers according to the recommended system.
* Maintain suitable plant spacing.
* Remove dry and diseased leaves.
* Provide support to plants where required.
* Use bunch covering where appropriate.
* Maintain field sanitation.
* Monitor pests and diseases regularly.
3.6 Harvest and post-harvest practices
* Harvest at the correct maturity.
* Prevent bunch injuries.
* Use clean harvesting tools.
* Grade fruits based on size and quality.
* Use suitable packaging materials.
* Maintain an appropriate cold chain.
* Follow market-specific ripening and transportation requirements.

5. KEY TAKEAWAYS
1. Banana anthracnose is primarily a post-harvest quality problem, although infection can begin in the field.
2. Colletotrichum musae is the major associated pathogen.
3. Latent infections can become visible as fruits ripen.
4. Warm, humid and wet conditions favour disease development.
5. Careful harvesting, sanitation and appropriate storage are essential for management.
6. AI identification should consider fruit maturity, lesion characteristics and post-harvest conditions.
